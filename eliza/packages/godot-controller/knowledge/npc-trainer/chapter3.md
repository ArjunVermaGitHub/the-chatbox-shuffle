# Social Etiquette Syllabus - Chapter 3: Dining Etiquette

Navigating a meal with others, whether formal or informal, involves a set of conventions that demonstrate respect and consideration. Knowing the basics helps everyone feel more comfortable.

**Key Concepts:**

1.  **Napkin Use:**

    - Place the napkin on your lap soon after being seated.
    - If you need to leave the table temporarily, loosely fold the napkin and place it on your chair (not the table).
    - Use it to dab your mouth, not wipe aggressively.
    - When finished, place the loosely folded napkin to the left of your plate.

2.  **Utensil Order:** In formal settings with multiple courses, utensils are often arranged outside-in. Start with the outermost fork and knife for the first course and work your way inwards. If unsure, discreetly observe the host or others. When finished with a course, place your knife and fork together on the plate (often diagonally, like pointing to 10 and 4 on a clock face) to signal you are done.

3.  **Holding Utensils:**

    - **American Style:** Cut food with knife in right hand, fork in left (tines down). Then, place knife on the plate edge, switch fork to right hand (tines up) to eat.
    - **Continental (European) Style:** Keep the fork in the left hand (tines down) and knife in the right throughout. Cut and lift food to mouth with the fork in the left hand.
    - Hold utensils gracefully, not gripped tightly like tools. Keep elbows off the table while eating.

4.  **Handling Food:**

    - Take moderate portions; you can always have seconds if offered.
    - Chew with your mouth closed and avoid talking with food in your mouth.
    - If you encounter something you dislike, discreetly move it to the side of your plate or simply leave it. No need to announce your dislike. For unfamiliar foods, take a small taste first.
    - Pass shared dishes (like bread baskets or salt/pepper) to the right. Pass salt and pepper together.

5.  **Interacting with Staff:** Be polite and respectful. Make eye contact when possible. Use "please" and "thank you." Address issues discreetly and calmly if necessary. Avoid snapping fingers or raising your voice to get attention.

6.  **Toasting:**

    - If someone proposes a toast, stop eating/talking and raise your glass.
    - Make eye contact with the person being toasted and/or the person giving the toast.
    - Take a sip (water or your beverage is fine) after the toast is concluded. It's generally not required to clink glasses, especially in large groups.
    - If you are the one being toasted, simply smile and acknowledge; do not drink to yourself.

7.  **The Bill & Tipping:** Follow the lead of the host if you are a guest. If splitting the bill, clarify how it will be done beforehand if possible. Understand local tipping customs – percentages and included service charges vary widely. Be prepared to contribute your share appropriately.

Dining etiquette is about making others feel comfortable around you while you eat. Relax, be considerate, and when in doubt, observe others.
